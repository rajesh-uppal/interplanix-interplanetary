# Interplanix Interplanetary

**Securing Space. Shaping Tomorrow.**

Interplanix Interplanetary is a research and development program focused on
trajectory intelligence, navigation, space situational awareness, and
infrastructure for operations beyond Earth orbit.

The program explores technologies supporting the evolution from Earth-orbit
operations to cislunar space, lunar operations, and eventually interplanetary
missions.

## Strategic Vision

Develop an integrated foundation for understanding, navigating, and managing
spacecraft and other objects across the Earth–Moon–Mars domain.

## Research and Development Areas

### 1. Cislunar Space Situational Awareness
- Tracking and characterization of objects in cislunar space
- Lunar orbital traffic awareness
- Trajectory monitoring and conjunction analysis

### 2. Trajectory Intelligence and Orbit Determination
- Observation-driven trajectory determination
- Catalog-assisted and hybrid navigation
- State estimation and uncertainty propagation
- Multi-body orbital dynamics

### 3. Multi-Sensor Tracking and Navigation
- Optical telescope observations
- Radar and lidar measurements where applicable
- Radio range and Doppler tracking
- Sensor fusion and measurement processing

### 4. Lunar Exploration and Operations
- Lunar orbital mapping
- Lunar spacecraft tracking and navigation
- Lunar mission planning
- Future lunar infrastructure concepts

### 5. Interplanetary Mission Technologies
- Earth–Moon–Mars trajectory analysis
- Deep-space navigation
- Autonomous mission planning
- Spacecraft digital twins and simulation

## Technical Principles

- Physics-based trajectory modelling
- Observation-driven orbit determination
- Explicit treatment of coordinate frames and time systems
- Uncertainty-aware state estimation
- Sensor-specific measurement models
- Validation against appropriate physical and numerical references

## Development Approach

The program will evolve through research, simulation, prototyping, and
engineering validation. Earth-orbit SSA capabilities provide a foundation
where applicable, but cislunar and interplanetary environments require
additional dynamical models, measurement approaches, and navigation methods.
